The short version

Private by default, minimal by design.

  • Your traffic is encrypted and its contents are never inspected or logged.
  • There are no accounts, ads, analytics, or trackers in VPNSky.
  • Your WireGuard private key is generated on your phone and never leaves it.
  • We keep only what the tunnel needs: a public key mapped to a private tunnel address.
Section 01

Who we are and what this covers

VPNSky is an Android VPN client developed and operated by an independent developer based in Indonesia (“VPNSky”, “we”, “us”, “our”). For the limited data described in this policy, the developer is the data controller.

This policy applies to the VPNSky Android application (package com.wingsheep.vpnsky), the registration service the app contacts to obtain a tunnel address, and this policy page. It does not cover Google Play, the websites and apps you reach through the tunnel, or any other third-party service. Those services have their own privacy policies.

VPNSky is not a social network and has no user accounts. We do not ask for your name, email address, phone number, or any other identifier in order to use the app.

Section 02

Data we handle

VPNSky is built around data minimisation. There is no profile and no advertising ID, and most of what the app needs never leaves your device. This table is the complete list of data the app and our service process.

Data What it is and where it lives Why we need it
WireGuard key pair A Curve25519 public/private key pair created on your device the first time you connect. The private key stays in the app's private storage; only the public key is sent to the VPNSky registry. Authenticates your installation to the server and lets the server assign it a tunnel address.
Tunnel address A private IPv4 address (for example 10.0.0.5/32) assigned to your public key. Stored in the registry's state file and used by the app for the connection. Routes your packets through the encrypted tunnel.
Connection metadata Your public IP address as seen by the VPN server, your public key, handshake timestamps, and byte counters. Visible to the server while you are connected; the registry records only the last registration time. Operate the tunnel, keep it stable, prevent abuse, and troubleshoot faults.
Configuration requests The app fetches server settings (endpoint, server public key, DNS, routing policy) from Firebase Remote Config. Google processes technical request data such as your IP address, app version, and a Firebase installation identifier. Deliver current server settings without shipping them in the app package.
Server location lookup After the tunnel is up, the app may resolve the VPN server's hostname with Google Public DNS and look up the server IP's city/country with ipwho.is. Only the server's address is sent — never your IP address, device ID, or a query about you. Show a readable server location in the app.
Access logs Our web infrastructure keeps standard access logs (IP address, time, requested path, user agent) for requests to the registry endpoint and to this page. Security, rate limiting, and debugging. Logs rotate automatically.
Support messages If you email us, we receive your email address and whatever you choose to include. To answer your question or request.

We do not combine any of the data above with data from other services, and we do not use it to build a profile of you.

Section 03

What we never collect

The app requests only the permissions a VPN needs. It cannot access, and does not ask for, any of the following:

  • Your name, email address, phone number, postal address, or government ID — there is no account.
  • Contacts, call logs, SMS/MMS, calendar, or other personal files.
  • Photos, videos, documents, or clipboard contents.
  • Precise or approximate location, including background location.
  • Camera, microphone, or screen contents.
  • Advertising ID or any identifier used to track you across apps and websites.
  • Browsing history, DNS query contents, or the contents of your network traffic.
  • Your installed-app list, keystrokes, device serial number, or IMEI.
  • Health, biometric, financial, or payment data — there are no in-app purchases.
  • Crash reports or diagnostics uploaded automatically — no crash-reporting SDK is included.
No advertising, analytics, attribution, or crash-reporting SDK ships in VPNSky. Nothing described in this policy is sold, rented, or shared with data brokers.
Section 04

How the VPN works — and what each side can see

A connection is a short exchange followed by an encrypted tunnel. This is the whole flight path:

  1. STEP 01 Key on device The app generates a fresh key pair in Android's private storage. The private key never leaves your phone.
  2. STEP 02 Register Over HTTPS, the app sends only the public key to the VPNSky registry, which maps it to a free tunnel address.
  3. STEP 03 Tunnel up The app builds a WireGuard configuration and asks Android for VPN permission. Packets are encrypted before they leave the device.
  4. STEP 04 Egress Our server decrypts inside the tunnel, forwards traffic to the internet, and returns replies encrypted to your device.

◉ What we can see

  • The connection metadata needed to run WireGuard: your IP address, public key, handshake timing, and byte counts.
  • At the network layer, the destination addresses of packets leaving the server.
  • Standard access-log entries when the app contacts our registry.

◎ What we never see

  • The contents of your TLS/HTTPS sessions — exactly as on any network.
  • Your WireGuard private key.
  • Your identity: there is no account, name, or email attached to the tunnel.
  • Traffic from apps that never send data through the tunnel.

We do not log, store, inspect, or analyse browsing activity, DNS queries, or payload contents. The VPN server forwards packets; it does not keep a history of them.

Full tunnel by default. The app routes all IPv4 and IPv6 traffic (0.0.0.0/0, ::/0) through the tunnel unless the operator narrows the policy. DNS requests travel inside the tunnel to the resolver configured for the service — currently Cloudflare's 1.1.1.1 by default — so your mobile network does not see them.

Section 07

How long we keep data

We keep data only as long as it is needed for the purpose it was collected for.

WireGuard private key
Until you clear the app's data, reinstall the app, rotate the key, or uninstall. It exists only on your device.
Registry mapping
The public key ↔ tunnel address mapping and its last registration time are kept while your address is allocated. When the address pool is full, the least recently active peer is recycled and its mapping is replaced. We do not keep a history of past sessions.
WireGuard runtime counters
Held in the server's memory only, reset when the tunnel or service restarts, and never archived.
Access logs
Rotated automatically and kept for a short operational period — typically no more than 30 days.
Support email
Kept while we handle your request and for a reasonable follow-up period, then deleted. You can ask us to delete it sooner.
Traffic contents, browsing history, DNS logs
Never stored.

If you ask us to remove your tunnel record, we delete the public key and assigned address. Your device simply registers a new key the next time you connect.

Section 08

How we protect data

Security is the product, so it gets the same scrutiny as the tunnel itself.

  • Modern cryptography. WireGuard uses ChaCha20-Poly1305, Curve25519, BLAKE2s, and HKDF. Each installation has its own keys; no shared secret is shipped in the app package.
  • Keys stay on the device. The private key lives in Android app-private storage, is excluded from Android backups (android:allowBackup="false"), and is never logged or sent to the registry.
  • Encrypted in transit. Registration and configuration requests use HTTPS/TLS.
  • A small server surface. The registry runs as an unprivileged service behind a narrow sudo rule, requires a service token, and its code is root-owned. Deployments use a restricted helper rather than broad shell access.
  • Least privilege on Android. The app requests only internet, network-state, and VPN foreground-service permissions — nothing else.
  • Tunnel lifecycle. The VPN service starts only when you connect and stops when you disconnect, with a visible ongoing notification while it runs.

No system is perfectly secure. If you find a vulnerability, please email us. We welcome coordinated disclosure and will credit researchers who ask to be credited.

We will never ask for your private key, your password, or payment details. VPNSky has no payments and no passwords. Anyone who asks for them is not us.
Section 09

Your choices and rights

No account to manage

VPNSky has no login. Uninstalling the app removes the private key and local settings from your device. Clearing the app's data has the same effect on the key.

Remove your tunnel record

You can ask us to delete the registry mapping for your installation. Email us with the tunnel address shown in the app while connected (or the approximate date you last connected) and we will remove it. No other proof of identity is required, because no identity was ever collected.

Your legal rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to:

  • access the personal data we hold about you, and receive a copy;
  • correct inaccurate data;
  • delete your data (the right to erasure);
  • restrict or object to processing;
  • withdraw consent where processing is based on consent — none of our current processing depends on consent; and
  • complain to a data protection authority.

If you are in the EEA or UK, these rights come from the GDPR and UK GDPR. If you are in California, the CCPA/CPRA gives you the rights to know, delete, correct, and opt out of sale or sharing; we do not sell or share personal information, and we will not discriminate against you for exercising your rights. If you are in Indonesia, equivalent rights apply under the Personal Data Protection Law (UU 27/2022).

To exercise any right, email daniootsutsuki@gmail.com. We may ask for the minimum information needed to locate your record — usually just the tunnel address. We respond within 30 days (45 days for California requests) and will tell you if we need more time.

Device controls

You can disconnect or revoke the VPN at any time from the app, or from Android Settings → Network & internet → VPN. Android will show a persistent notification while the tunnel is active, and you can stop it from there as well.

Section 10

Children's privacy

VPNSky is a general-purpose privacy tool and is not directed to children under 13 (or under 16 in the EEA/UK where local law sets a higher age). We do not knowingly collect personal data from children: there is no account, no advertising, and no profiling.

If you believe a child has used the app and you want the tunnel record removed, contact us and we will delete it. Parents and guardians can also uninstall the app to remove all local data immediately.

Section 11

International transfers

VPNSky's server and service providers may be located outside your country. The VPN endpoint currently in use is shown in the app while you are connected. Google processes Firebase and DNS requests through its global infrastructure.

Where data leaves the EEA, UK, or Switzerland, we rely on adequacy decisions or standard contractual clauses (including the UK Addendum) with our providers. The data involved is limited to technical identifiers and connection metadata; there is no account data to transfer.
